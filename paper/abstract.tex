Adaptive noise cancellation with a reference microphone is a classic use of the least-mean-square (LMS)
family, and a small field-programmable gate array (FPGA) can run it at audio rates without a processor.
This paper presents ULLAP, a 12-bit, 32-tap noise canceller for the DE2-115 board (Cyclone IV E) with a
WM8731 codec. One serial core with a single shared multiplier implements LMS, normalised LMS (NLMS) with a
restoring divider, and NLMS-Shift, which replaces the division by a leading-one detector and a shift. With
step sizes tuned on held-out seeds, NLMS-Shift matches NLMS within the 95\% confidence interval on
stationary and amplitude-step noise (22.37 and 24.33~dB over 20 seeds) and is about 2~dB better than LMS
on amplitude steps, while needing 72 instead of 130 busy cycles per sample. NLMS-Shift is shown to equal
NLMS with a level-dependent step between 0.5 and 1 times the nominal one. On real recorded noise and speech
the filters remove 11.7--14.9~dB and raise the short-time objective intelligibility from 0.69--0.78 to
0.95--0.98; without speech they remove 18.5--31.9~dB, so the speech, not the noise, limits them. A
12-bit word keeps the quantisation loss below 0.4~dB on broadband noise. On the board, all 724,459 captured output samples
of the three algorithms equal the integer model's, and the measured sample rate, latency and busy cycles
match the register-transfer-level design.
