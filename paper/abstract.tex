Adaptive noise cancellation with a reference microphone is a classical application of the
least-mean-square (LMS) family, and a small field-programmable gate array (FPGA) can perform it at audio
rates without any processor. This paper presents ULLAP, a 12-bit, 32-tap noise canceller for the DE2-115
board (Cyclone IV E) and its WM8731 codec. One serial core with a single shared multiplier implements LMS,
normalized LMS (NLMS) with a restoring divider, and NLMS-Shift, in which a leading-one detector and a
shift take the place of the division. With step sizes tuned on held-out seeds, NLMS-Shift matches NLMS
within the 95\% confidence interval on stationary and amplitude-step noise (22.37 and 24.33~dB over 20
seeds), outperforms LMS by about 2~dB on amplitude steps, and needs 72 rather than 130 busy cycles per
sample. NLMS-Shift is also shown to be equivalent to NLMS with a level-dependent step between 0.5 and 1
times the nominal step. On real recorded noise and speech, the filters remove 11.7--14.9~dB and raise
short-time objective intelligibility from 0.69--0.78 to 0.95--0.98; without speech they remove
18.5--31.9~dB, so the speech, not the noise, limits performance. A 12-bit word keeps the quantization loss
below 0.4~dB on broadband noise. On the board, all 724,459 captured output samples of the three
algorithms equal those of the integer model, and the measured sample rate, latency and busy cycles agree
with the register-transfer-level design.
