Adaptive noise cancellation with a reference microphone is a classical application of the
least-mean-square (LMS) family, and a small field-programmable gate array (FPGA) can perform it at audio
rates without a processor. In this paper, ULLAP, a 12-bit, 32-tap noise canceller, is presented for the
DE2-115 board (Cyclone IV E) with a WM8731 codec. A single serial core with one shared multiplier
implements LMS, normalized LMS (NLMS) with a restoring divider, and NLMS-Shift, in which the division is
replaced by a leading-one detector and a shift. With the step sizes tuned on held-out seeds, NLMS-Shift
matches NLMS within the 95\% confidence interval on stationary and amplitude-step noise (22.37 and
24.33~dB over 20 seeds) and outperforms LMS by about 2~dB on amplitude steps, while needing 72 instead of
130 busy cycles per sample. Moreover, NLMS-Shift is shown to be equal to NLMS with a level-dependent step
between 0.5 and 1 times the nominal step. On real recorded noise and speech, the filters remove
11.7--14.9~dB and raise the short-time objective intelligibility from 0.69--0.78 to 0.95--0.98, whereas
without speech they remove 18.5--31.9~dB, which shows that the speech, and not the noise, limits the
performance. A 12-bit word keeps the quantization loss below 0.4~dB on broadband noise. On the board, all
724,459 captured output samples of the three algorithms are equal to those of the integer model, and the
measured sample rate, latency and busy cycles match the register-transfer-level design.
