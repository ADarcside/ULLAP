Adaptive noise cancellation with a reference input is a classic application of the least-mean-square
(LMS) family, and small FPGAs can run it at audio rates without a processor. Published FPGA
implementations, however, rarely compare algorithm variants under equal tuning effort, and few show
that the hardware reproduces the reference model. This paper presents ULLAP, a parameterisable
12-bit, 32-tap noise canceller for the Terasic DE2-115 (Cyclone IV E) with a WM8731 codec,
supporting LMS, NLMS with a restoring divider, and NLMS-Shift, in which the normalisation is a
leading-one detection and a barrel shift. All three share one time-multiplexed multiplier and are
verified bit-exact against an integer model in 86 regression tests, including the complete
codec interface. With step sizes tuned on held-out seeds, NLMS-Shift matches NLMS within the 95\%
confidence interval on stationary and amplitude-step noise (22.37 and 24.33~dB final noise
reduction over 20 seeds) and is about 2~dB better than LMS on amplitude steps, while needing 72
instead of 130 busy cycles per sample and 16\% fewer register-transfer-level bit toggles than NLMS.
On real recorded noise and speech at 0~dB input SNR, the three algorithms remove 11.7--14.9~dB of noise and raise STOI from 0.69--0.78 to 0.95--0.98 and narrow-band PESQ from 1.4--1.5 to 2.3--2.8, with NLMS 0.4--0.6~dB ahead of LMS and NLMS-Shift between them. Without speech the same filters remove 18.5--31.9~dB, so the speech, not the noise,
limits them. NLMS-Shift is shown to equal NLMS with a level-dependent step between 0.5 and 1 times the nominal one.
A word-length study at the tuned settings shows 11 bits is the shortest word within 3~dB of floating
point and 12 bits keeps the loss below 0.4~dB on broadband noise. All variants close timing at
50~MHz. On the board, 25 Signal Tap captures of real codec samples show every one of 724,459 output
samples of the three algorithms equal to the integer model's, the freeze switch and the DAC word behave
as modelled, and the measured sample rate, latency and busy cycles match the RTL, also after
10~minutes of operation. On the simulation signals the board removes 14.0--17.1~dB of synthetic noise,
which on stationary and path-switch noise equals the model within 0.2~dB at the 3~dB lower input level
the board received.
